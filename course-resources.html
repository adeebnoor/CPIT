Official course specification

CourseCPIT-455 · Software Engineering (II)
PrerequisiteCPIT-251
Primary textIan Sommerville · Software Engineering · 10th ed.

Catalog focus

Advanced software-engineering principles and techniques spanning specification, design, testing, software teams, development cost, quality management, software processes and reconstruction/maintenance-related management.

Why this lecture sequence?

The official coverage lists Dependable Systems, Reliability, Safety, Security, Resilience, Software Reuse, Component-Based Software Engineering, Distributed Software Engineering and Systems of Systems. That is why the hub deliberately covers Chapters 10–17 and then Chapter 20 rather than presenting a continuous chapter-number sequence.

Course Learning Outcomes

CLO 1Design dependability in systems using redundancy and diversity.
CLO 2Write functional and non-functional dependability and security requirements.
CLO 3Apply reliability metrics to specify measurable reliability requirements.
CLO 4Design safety in safety-critical systems.
CLO 5Design and test secure systems.
CLO 6Design systems to support resilience.
CLO 7Identify elements involved in software reuse and describe standardized reusable-component approaches.
CLO 8Describe software reuse through composition of standardized reusable components.
CLO 9Explain distributed-systems engineering and distributed-system architectures.
CLO 10Explain systems of systems and discuss challenges in building complex software-intensive systems.
CLO 11Design and implement test cases across different testing strategies.

Current semester operating model

Five observable outcomes

  1. Explain dependability as trust across reliability, availability, safety, security and resilience.
  2. Trace a failure across technical and sociotechnical layers.
  3. Compare alternatives and name the trade-off.
  4. Build a bounded verdict with inspectable evidence.
  5. Defend the verdict when a constraint changes.

Decision principle

The course treats the semester as a decision system rather than a chapter list. Students are expected to expose assumptions, identify constraints, inspect evidence, surface counter-evidence and own the final decision.

Human sign-off: AI may prepare drafts, tests, summaries, probes, candidate code or evidence structures. It must not own certification, approval, risk acceptance or final assurance.
