STUDENT & INSTRUCTOR GUIDE

One familiar rhythm.
A new engineering challenge.

The original concepts stay visible. iSCARB supplies the reasoning practice around them.

Navigate the visual lectures

  1. Start with the case. Identify the decision and what remains unknown.
  2. Follow the visual overview. Each concept has a diagram or source figure and a short takeaway.
  3. Open Details when you need the explanation. Back and Next move through numbered pages before the next concept. Use Units to jump.
  4. Attempt the checks before revealing answers. Complete the named reading and apply its concept in the assignment.

Arrow keys and Page Up / Page Down also navigate. Tab moves between controls. Local saving remains separate from Blackboard submission.

Required self-study: exact scope and evidence

  1. Open Required self-study in each chapter. It names two source selections, generally 8–12 slides, and links to each original slide.
  2. Complete the five-objective preparation check before the chapter assignment and next discussion. Blackboard supplies calendar dates. Review errors and retry; this local practice is not an LMS grade.
  3. Apply one assigned source concept to your artifact, then answer the short transfer question. These are assessed within the existing rubric totals.
  4. Be ready to explain a changed example orally. The instructor samples students over the course using a stated rubric; public practice questions are not a secure exam.

Assessment scope: the five chapter objectives, classroom concepts and named required self-study. The full source ledger supports clarification. Optional toolkit activities are enrichment unless specifically assigned. Additional required source topics must be announced in Blackboard before assessment.

Workload and feedback

Initial planning budget: 15–25 minutes for reading plus the preparation check, followed by compact assignment work; approximately 35–55 minutes total, or 45–65 minutes in Chapters 16 and 17. Report actual time. These are planning targets to adjust, not measured completion times.

Use about 180–260 focused words in the complete written response; tables and executable logs replace paragraphs. The two local labs are part of the existing assignments, not two extra assignments.

Submit once through Blackboard. After instructor feedback, complete the optional feedback/revision fields, correct or defend your final record and export the revised copy when requested. The first prepared export is preserved locally. Download backups; this page cannot confirm LMS submission.

Already started? Use the previous-edition link inside the current assignment to resume or export old work with the same ID and browser. Do not redo a completed submission unless the instructor explicitly requests it. Grades and deadlines remain those announced in Blackboard.

For students

  1. Open the chapter route. Read the five objectives and the fictional case. The classroom sequence includes the original foundations and three short interactions.
  2. Try before revealing. Answer the two short checks and the application task in your own words. Open the example explanation afterward.
  3. Use the source map. A topic studied after class remains required. Each topic links to its classroom location and the original source slides.
  4. Complete the separate assignment. Keep the initial judgment, commit it, inspect STRESS, then justify RETAIN, REVISE or REPLACE.
  5. Review and submit. Declare AI assistance, check every claim, export your work and submit through the LMS.

For instructors

Begin with the chapter goal and case, then teach the source concepts in order. Use only three planned interaction stops: Check 1, Check 2 and Apply it. Per-page questions can serve self-study without becoming another in-class task.

For chapters allocated two weeks in the supplied syllabus, use two blocks and pause after Check 2. For one-week chapters, plan 75–90 minutes of explanation and the named reading and preparation check, using the total-workload targets below. These are estimates: preserve your actual timetable.

If a required concept is unfinished, schedule it for the next block or explicitly assign its source review. Do not treat the toolkit as a second compulsory lecture.

Use N for the current unit’s teaching notes. Use the source map to record what remains to be taught in your LMS. The website records material availability, not attendance or teaching completion.

Controls and saved work

Use Next/Back or the arrow keys. The Units menu separates the classroom route, source review and toolkit. A−/A+ change reading size. Card opens the practice artifact; Download saves the standalone lecture. Source map links and unit URLs let you return to a specific concept.

Lecture work is local to this browser. Assignment drafts are separated by chapter and Student ID. Neither is automatically sent to the instructor. Download a backup before leaving a shared device. The assignment’s commit sequence preserves your first answer for teaching purposes; it is not a secure examination system.

What stays consistent

CRISIS → MAP → TRADE-OFF → EVIDENCE → VERDICT remains the reasoning thread. The six-field Decision Card remains available. The assignments keep FIT → BOUND → ACT + EVIDENCE → STRESS → REFIT, with one chapter-specific artifact step from Assignment 2 onward.

Readiness checks support reflection. Filled fields, reviewed pages and practice scores do not certify that an explanation is correct. The instructor judges the reasoning.
