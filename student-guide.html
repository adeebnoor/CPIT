STUDENT & INSTRUCTOR GUIDE

One familiar rhythm.
A new engineering challenge.

The original concepts stay visible. iSCARB supplies the reasoning practice around them.

For students

  1. Open the chapter route. Read the five objectives and the fictional case. The classroom sequence includes the original foundations and three short interactions.
  2. Try before revealing. Answer the two short checks and the application task in your own words. Open the example explanation afterward.
  3. Use the source map. A topic studied after class remains required. Each topic links to its classroom location and the original source slides.
  4. Complete the separate assignment. Keep the initial judgment, commit it, inspect STRESS, then justify RETAIN, REVISE or REPLACE.
  5. Review and submit. Declare AI assistance, check every claim, export your work and submit through the LMS.

For instructors

Begin with the chapter goal and case, then teach the source concepts in order. Use only three planned interaction stops: Check 1, Check 2 and Apply it. Per-page questions can serve self-study without becoming another in-class task.

For chapters allocated two weeks in the supplied syllabus, use two blocks and pause after Check 2. For one-week chapters, plan 75–90 minutes of explanation and 20–30 minutes of targeted source review. These are estimates: preserve your actual timetable.

If a required concept is unfinished, schedule it for the next block or explicitly assign its source review. Do not treat the toolkit as a second compulsory lecture.

Use N for the current unit’s teaching notes. Use the source map to record what remains to be taught in your LMS. The website records material availability, not attendance or teaching completion.

Controls and saved work

Use Next/Back or the arrow keys. The Units menu separates the classroom route, source review and toolkit. A−/A+ change reading size. Card opens the practice artifact; Download saves the standalone lecture. Source map links and unit URLs let you return to a specific concept.

Lecture work is local to this browser. Assignment drafts are separated by chapter and Student ID. Neither is automatically sent to the instructor. Download a backup before leaving a shared device. The assignment’s commit sequence preserves your first answer for teaching purposes; it is not a secure examination system.

What stays consistent

CRISIS → MAP → TRADE-OFF → EVIDENCE → VERDICT remains the reasoning thread. The six-field Decision Card remains available. The assignments keep FIT → BOUND → ACT + EVIDENCE → STRESS → REFIT, with one chapter-specific artifact step from Assignment 2 onward.

Readiness checks support reflection. Filled fields, reviewed pages and practice scores do not certify that an explanation is correct. The instructor judges the reasoning.
