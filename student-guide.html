How to learn, decide, submit and defend.

ISCARB is not a second LMS. It is the learning and reasoning layer for CPIT-455. The LMS remains the official submission, deadline and feedback record. Your job is to turn course mechanisms into bounded engineering decisions that another professional can inspect.

1 · In-ClassLearn the disciplinary mechanism
2 · After-ClassApply FBR to a decision
3 · LMSSubmit the final artifact
4 · FeedbackReceive rubric + comments
5 · Verify / RefitDefend or revise when selected
THE CORE IDEA

Fact-Based Reasoning (FBR)

FITWhat mechanism or option is appropriate now, and why?
BOUNDWhere does that decision stop being fit?
ACTWhat concrete professional action or artifact follows?
EVIDENCEWhat makes the action independently defensible?
REFITAfter STRESS, retain, revise or replace the decision.
The point is not certainty. The point is a defensible boundary: a decision that states what is justified now, what evidence supports it, and what would make you reopen it.
SEQUENTIAL REASONING

What STRESS means

Before STRESS

Complete FIT → BOUND → ACT + EVIDENCE first. Commit Part A before you inspect the changed condition. This exposes your assumptions before the later information makes the answer feel obvious.

After STRESS

Ask whether the new condition crossed your stated boundary. Then explicitly choose RETAIN, REVISE, or REPLACE and explain the evidence behind that choice.

Integrity rule: do not inspect source code, hidden files, another student's STRESS condition, or post-commit material before you commit Part A. The public site cannot make STRESS cryptographically secret; the assessed sequence is therefore a professional-integrity rule.
EVALUATION

Weekly FBR rubric · 4 points

Criterion1 point · defensibleWeak / partial evidence
FITSelects the relevant mechanism or property and explains why it fits this specific use now.Names a concept but gives a generic definition or weak context link.
BOUNDStates an observable applicability boundary and a clear trigger for reopening the decision.Uses vague language such as “if things change” with no inspectable trigger.
ACT + EVIDENCEProduces a concrete professional action/artifact and names evidence another professional could inspect.Gives an intention without an actionable artifact, owner, measurement or evidence.
REFITExplains whether STRESS crossed the boundary, then rationally retains, revises or replaces the action.Changes the answer without referring to the boundary or ignores material contradictory evidence.

The score rewards defensible professional judgment—not length, confidence, polished English, or simply filling every box.

GENAI & ACADEMIC INTEGRITY

AI may assist. It may not own the judgment.

Permitted support

  • Language editing and clarity.
  • Brainstorming alternatives or counter-evidence.
  • Drafting tests, summaries, probes or candidate code.
  • Structuring evidence for review.

Human responsibility

  • You are responsible for every submitted FIT, BOUND, ACT, EVIDENCE and REFIT decision.
  • You must be able to explain and defend every sentence you submit.
  • AI must not own certification, approval, risk acceptance or final assurance.
  • If AI surfaces unclear counter-evidence, pause, escalate and verify independently.
Required declaration in the LMS comment: AI use: None / Language-editing / Brainstorming / Other: ____. “Human sign-off” means you accept accountability for the correctness, limitations and consequences of the submitted analysis.

FEEDBACK & ORAL VERIFICATION

What happens after submission?

1 · MARKInstructor applies the published FBR criteria.
2 · VERIFYSelected students complete a short micro-viva / oral verification.
3 · FEED FORWARDUse feedback to improve the next bounded decision.
A viva is not a memory test. It checks ownership: can you explain your claim, evidence, boundary, counter-evidence and why your final verdict is still justified?
