CRISIS · Start with the case01 / 20 · 2 min

Reliability Engineering

Constructed teaching scenario · Chapter 11 · 2 minutes

A Saudi service supplier offers “99.9% reliability.” The contract does not define a failure, the observation window, user workload, restoration time or treatment of degraded service. The number is now awaiting sign-off.

Predict · 30 seconds

Is “99.9% reliability” ready for acceptance?

Show answer & explanation
No. State the service, failure event, metric, observation interval and workload. If the intended metric is availability, define downtime, degraded operation and exclusions. A precise-looking number can still be an untestable requirement.

Teaching scenario; not a report of a real incident.

Reliability and availability answer different questions

Always state the service boundary and observation period.
Pause & discuss

Can a frequently failing service have high availability?

Show answer & explanation
Yes, if interruptions are very short. The same service may have a poor probability of completing a long uninterrupted session.

MAP · Concept & visual05 / 20 · 3 min

Choose the metric to match the service

POFOD is per demand. ROCOF is a rate. Neither is a percentage of uptime.
Pause & discuss

Which metric best describes an emergency shutdown that is rarely demanded?

Show answer & explanation
Probability of failure on demand (POFOD). Define what counts as a demand, failure and representative test or operating context.

A protection service fails on 2 of 10,000 demands. Its observed POFOD is:

MAP · Concept & visual07 / 20 · 3 min

Repair time changes availability

For a steady alternating up/down model: A = MTTF / (MTTF + MTTR). Define any use of “MTBF” explicitly.
Pause & discuss

If downtime becomes 30 hours with the same 990 hours up, what is availability?

Show answer & explanation
990 / 1020 = 0.970588, about 97.06%. The calculation assumes the observation boundary contains exactly those up and down intervals.

MAP · Concept & visual08 / 20 · 3 min

A requirement needs a test protocol

A quality target is testable only when its meaning and measurement are testable.
Pause & discuss

What does a 99.9% availability target allow over a 30-day month?

Show answer & explanation
A 30-day month contains 43,200 minutes. At 0.1% unavailable time, the allowance is 43.2 minutes, assuming continuous service and no excluded intervals.

TRADE-OFF · Study an example10 / 20 · 4 min

Worked example: an appointment service

Worked example · try, then reveal

Does this observation meet a 99.9% monthly availability target?

Show answer & explanation
The window is 30 × 24 × 60 = 43,200 minutes. Availability = (43,200 − 30) / 43,200 = 99.9306%, so the observation meets 99.9%. It does not establish future reliability or a failure-free session probability.

Model reasoning for this practice case. Different choices can be defensible under different stated constraints.

The same service has 60 minutes of downtime next month, also 30 days. Does it meet the target?

Show answer & explanation
Availability = 43,140 / 43,200 = 99.8611%; it misses 99.9%. The maximum allowance for this defined month is 43.2 minutes.

TRADE-OFF · Deepen the mechanism12 / 20 · 3 min

Defensive programming limits propagation

Recovery code is part of the trusted behavior and must itself be tested.
Pause & discuss

Should an unexpected exception simply be ignored to keep the service up?

Show answer & explanation
No. Ignoring it can continue with corrupt state. Use a defined containment or recovery strategy, log the event and preserve required invariants.

TRADE-OFF · Evaluate the cost14 / 20 · 3 min

Test with a representative operational profile

A test dominated by rare operations can misrepresent routine service, and vice versa.
Pause & discuss

A test workload contains 95% reads, but production contains 70% writes. Can its result be transferred directly?

Show answer & explanation
No. The operational profiles differ, so the measured estimate does not directly describe production. Reweight or redesign testing using justified workload evidence.

EVIDENCE · Verify the claim15 / 20 · 2 min

Make the evidence inspectable

AI can check arithmetic and draft test cases. Recompute the metric and validate the event definitions and operational profile yourself.
Discuss · evidence and AI

Which evidence item would most directly support or overturn your case decision?

Show answer & explanation
Use an artifact linked to the governing requirement and the current case boundary. Metric definition: Failure event, denominator and time window; Measurement: Timestamped failures and restoration records; Representativeness: Workload and environment match the claim. Name what result would make you reopen the verdict. A document title without its relevant result is insufficient.

EVIDENCE · Collaborate & defend16 / 20 · 4 min

Stress the decision together

Collaborative activity · rotate roles

Repair time triples while failure frequency is unchanged. What needs revision?

Show answer & explanation
Availability is likely to decrease under the same model. Recalculate using the new repair assumption, review restoration capacity and revise the availability acceptance argument. Do not automatically change an unrelated POFOD estimate.

Retain, revise or replace only when the reasoning warrants it. A changed fact does not mechanically require a changed verdict.

EVIDENCE · Bound the assurance17 / 20 · 3 min

Zero observed failures is not proof of perfection

Reliability growth depends on fixing faults without introducing compensating faults.
Pause & discuss

What must accompany a reliability estimate?

Show answer & explanation
Exposure, the failure definition, the operational profile, the estimation assumptions and uncertainty. A point estimate alone hides how much evidence supports the claim.
