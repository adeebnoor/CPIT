1 · WARM-UP · 5 MINUTES · NOT GRADED

Start with one clear distinction.

An online timetable opens whenever requested, but sometimes displays yesterday’s timetable. Is availability alone enough to call it dependable?

Show / hide warm-up answer
No. Availability concerns whether the service is accessible when needed; reliability concerns correct service over time under stated conditions. A page can be accessible while returning incorrect information. State what users need and what evidence would establish correct behaviour.

No written warm-up response is required. Now transfer the reasoning to a different service in Part A.

2 · PART A · 15 MINUTES

Assessed scenario · campus course registration

At 11:30, a fictional university's course-registration portal is experiencing intermittent errors during peak registration. Registration closes at 12:00. Two application servers support the portal, and automatic failover is enabled. The available failover test passed during a quiet period; no peak-load failover result is available. Current checks show that students can sign in and that confirmed registrations can be retrieved. No lost or duplicate registrations have been confirmed, but this has not yet been comprehensively checked. You are the duty software engineer. The registrar owns the registration deadline and may authorize an alternative submission route; you cannot change either policy yourself.

Your decision: Recommend one immediate operating plan: continue with stated controls, limit operation, or pause the affected function. Start with one governing dependability property, one observable boundary, one action with a responsible role, and one inspectable evidence item. Explain any mechanism you rely on. State what is known and what you would still need to verify. Your plan must address students' ability to complete registration and the integrity of confirmed registrations. There is no single required choice.

Teaching simulation. Produce a decision record using the stated facts. Do not invent test results or certify a real service. Mark missing evidence as unmeasured and specify how you would obtain it.

Aim for 2–4 focused sentences per reasoning field, about 250–400 focused words overall as guidance. ACT can be a compact record with labelled lines. Include at least one relevant lecture unit/title or assigned source-page citation supporting your mechanism. No code, external experiments, real student records or numerical reliability calculation is required.

A1 · FIT · 1 POINT

Select the leading dependability property, explain the consequence for users, and justify one relevant mechanism. Distinguish the property from the mechanism used to support it.

A2 · BOUND · 1 POINT

State an observable applicability condition and a trigger for reopening the decision. Name what remains unknown. Label any threshold you propose as proposed and requiring owner approval. Do not guess the later STRESS.

A3 · ACT · SCORED WITH EVIDENCE

State the action or verdict, its reason and operating condition, and the role responsible for it. This is your actual short artifact, not a promise to write one later.

A4 · EVIDENCE · SCORED WITH ACT

Name a record, test or measurement; say why it supports the decision, which version or conditions it must cover, and who checks it. Distinguish supplied scenario facts, assumptions, and evidence you still need. Label proposed tests or logs as requested evidence, not completed results. BOUND is the condition; EVIDENCE is how you check it.
