1 · WARM-UP · 5 MINUTES · NOT GRADED

A high uptime number can still hide the wrong failure.

A payment service was reachable for 99.99% of a test, but some payment requests returned an error. Does the availability number alone establish reliability of payment authorization?

Show / hide warm-up answer
No. Availability asks whether the service is accessible; reliability asks whether it delivers the required service correctly under stated conditions. For a high-volume transaction service, a failure-rate measure such as ROCOF can be more informative for the authorization function. The metric must match the service, demand pattern and failure definition.

No written warm-up response is required. Assignment 2 adds only one new burden: make the reliability claim measurable before you defend the operating decision.

Lecture guide and sentence stems

Use the lecture units on reliability vs availability, POFOD / ROCOF / AVAIL, quantitative reliability requirements, fault-tolerant architectures, operational profiles, Rule 15 — AI assist + human sign-off, and the Decision Card.

What distinguishes an unsupported answer from a stronger one?

Different practice example · weak: “The test passed, so reliability is guaranteed.”

Stronger: In a different example, 2 failures in 1,000 transactions gives 0.002 failures per transaction. This estimates the observed sample under its profile; it is not a confidence bound or a guarantee about production.

The stronger answer names a mechanism, its scope and inspectable evidence. It does not certify a real system. Your assessed answer must address this task’s own facts.

2 · PART A · 20 MINUTES

Assessed scenario · fictional university payment authorization service

A candidate release of a fictional university payment-authorization service will be used during registration week. The service handles many short, similar requests. The stated non-functional requirement is: ROCOF shall be no greater than 0.0005 authorization failures per payment transaction during registration service hours.

In the latest reliability test, the candidate processed 20,000 payment-authorization transactions and logged 6 authorization failures. The service was reachable for 99.98% of the test period. Two application instances run behind automatic failover. The test report says the dataset is “registration-like,” but the detailed operational-profile distribution is not included in the brief you received. No duplicate confirmed payments were found in the inspected sample, but the duplicate-payment audit is not yet complete.

You are the duty software engineer preparing a recommendation to the service owner. Choose one immediate action: release, release with explicit restrictions/conditions, or hold. Your recommendation must distinguish what the measured data supports from what remains unverified.

Teaching simulation. Use only the supplied facts. You may calculate from them. Do not invent additional test results, thresholds or completed audits. If you propose extra evidence or a new threshold, label it proposed.

Keep the complete written response to about 180–260 focused words; use compact tables or notation. Include the source application and transfer answer in that budget. No external experiment is required. State facts, assumptions and proposed evidence separately. Quote or cite the assigned source precisely; explain its role. Report actual study time at the end.

A1 · FIT · 1 POINT

Define what counts as failure for the payment-authorization service. Select the primary metric (POFOD, ROCOF/MTTF, or AVAIL) and explain why it fits this usage pattern. You may mention a secondary metric, but identify one primary metric.

A2 · MEASURE · 1 POINT

Calculate the observed authorization failure rate from the supplied numbers. Compare it with the stated ROCOF requirement. Then state one sentence about what this comparison does not prove.

A3 · BOUND · 1 POINT

State the operational/test conditions your conclusion depends on and a trigger that would make you reopen it. Include at least one uncertainty visible in the scenario.

A4 · ACT + EVIDENCE · 1 POINT

State release / restricted release / hold, the condition behind it, one reliability mechanism you rely on, and the responsible role.

Name the relevant test/log/audit, the conditions it must cover, who checks it, and what evidence is still missing. Do not describe requested evidence as if it already exists.
