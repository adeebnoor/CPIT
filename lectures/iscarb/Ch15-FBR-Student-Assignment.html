1 · WARM-UP · 5 MINUTES · NOT GRADED

A purchased system costs less to acquire than a custom build. Is it necessarily the cheaper option over its useful life?

Show / hide warm-up answer

No. Integration, configuration, support, upgrades, migration and exit constraints affect the lifecycle comparison. Do not invent missing costs.

Learning-outcome alignment

This task supports supplied syllabus CLO 7. It practises disciplinary knowledge, engineering skills and accountable decisions. It is course-written practice, not an official national readiness assessment or an approved item-weight mapping.

What distinguishes an unsupported answer from a stronger one?

Different practice example · weak: “Choose the product with more features.”

Stronger: For a different configurable package, required lifetime exceeds documented support. Record that mismatch and the exit-data unknown; compare a bounded evaluation with the alternative rather than inventing costs.

The stronger answer names a mechanism, its scope and inspectable evidence. It does not certify a real system. Your assessed answer must address this task’s own facts.

2 · PART A · 20 MINUTES

Assessed scenario · fictional teaching case

Task format: Critique a colleague’s recommendation. The same five criteria apply.

A fictional faculty needs a room-booking service for a three-year pilot. Option A is a configurable commercial product with the required booking functions and an export API; its support commitment ends after two years. Option B is an existing university application with an owned codebase, but it lacks recurring bookings and has no measured peak-load results. The team knows the university codebase. No reliable total-cost estimates have been supplied. The target is a pilot next term, not a complete enterprise replacement. Recommend an option, a restricted evaluation or a hold.

Colleague’s draft recommendation · review it, do not copy it: “Choose Option A for the whole faculty from next term. It already has every booking function we need, so configuration is enough. Support ending after two years is not a problem for a three-year pilot: we will simply renew. Option B would take too long to finish, and the team’s knowledge of it does not change that. Total cost is clearly lower for A because it is ready.”

Teaching simulation. Use only the supplied facts. You may calculate from them. Do not invent additional test results, thresholds or completed audits. If you propose extra evidence or a new threshold, label it proposed.

Keep the written response to about 120–180 focused words; use compact tables or notation. Include the source application and transfer answer in that budget. Your Python code, tests and build record are not counted in it. State facts, assumptions and proposed evidence separately. Quote or cite the assigned source precisely; explain its role. Report actual study time at the end.

A1 · FIT · 1 POINT

Classify the two reuse approaches and the required functions and constraints. Then state where the colleague’s draft frames the problem wrongly: scope, lifecycle or requirement.

PYTHON BUILD · ASSESSED IN COMPARE

Build the evidence-only fit check

Write fit, shortlist and evidence_needed from the supplied facts only, so that nothing unknown can count as met. Use the output to test each claim in the colleague’s draft.

First time? Try the not-assessed practice build and a complete worked example on How to do an assignment.

Budget about 30 minutes. Python runs in your browser: nothing to install, and nothing is uploaded. The first run downloads Python once (about 10 MB, internet needed). Passing every check is necessary, not sufficient: your written record must use what the build shows.

Your own tests are tested too: the course runs them against known buggy versions of this code, and each bug must make one of your tests fail. Your build record names a micro-viva change request chosen from your Student ID. Be ready to make that change in front of your instructor and explain which tests change and why.

Primary source · about 15 minutes: D. Garlan, R. Allen and J. Ockerbloom, “Architectural Mismatch: Why Reuse Is So Hard,” IEEE Software 12(6), 1995. Read it for the assumptions a reused component makes that its description does not state. Which of your “unknown” results is such an assumption? Use the answer in your build plan.

The course checks your build must pass
  • supplied facts are reported as given
  • unsettled requirements are unknown, never met
  • the shortlist needs evidence for every requirement
  • evidence_needed lists exactly the unknowns
  • your own tests: at least three, all passing
  • your tests catch every known bug (mutation test)

Keep the given names. Write at least three functions whose names start with test_. Tab inserts four spaces.

Write your plan, then run.



A2 · COMPARE · 1 POINT
Build the comparison the draft skipped: four reuse-planning factors, each marked supported, unknown or dependent on a condition. Flag each claim in the draft that the supplied facts do not support. Do not invent scores, prices or performance.


A3 · BOUND · 1 POINT
Identify the lifecycle assumption the draft relies on without evidence, and a specific change that would reverse your own recommendation.


A4 · ACT + EVIDENCE · 1 POINT
Write the recommendation you would sign instead of the draft: a bounded selection or evaluation, its owner, and the evidence that would settle one consequential unknown.
Name the relevant test/log/audit, the conditions it must cover, who checks it, and what evidence is still missing. Do not describe requested evidence as if it already exists.




ASSIGNED SOURCE APPLICATION · INCLUDED IN THE EXISTING RUBRIC
Write the source slide number, explain the concept in your own words, and identify the specific part of your artifact it supports or challenges. Use the assigned pages above. A citation alone is insufficient.
TRANSFER CHECK · SCORED WITH FIT
A framework invokes your extension code; a library is called by your program. Explain the control difference and one consequence for integrating an existing component.
