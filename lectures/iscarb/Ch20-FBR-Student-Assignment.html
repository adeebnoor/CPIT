How your work is assessed · 5 points

CriterionScoring
FIT · 1 point1: Defines the chapter-specific problem accurately, distinguishes facts from assumptions and connects it to the proposed action. The short transfer answer must correctly apply the second concept; FIT and transfer together earn this single point.
0.75: The right concept and mechanism, but one link is thin: a fact and an assumption are not separated, or the transfer answer applies the second concept only partly.
0.5: A relevant concept is named but applied generically or to the wrong mechanism, or the transfer answer is missing or incorrect.
0: No chapter concept is applied, the case is misread, or the answer depends on invented facts. A generic “hold and test” statement does not satisfy this criterion.
INTEGRATE · 1 point1: A feasible cross-owner agreement and staged acceptance test that handles incomplete participation and makes assumptions and authority explicit. Apply one named required source concept to the artifact and explain the connection.
0.75: A feasible agreement and staged test, but authority or incomplete participation is handled only partly—for example, no fallback when one owner does not take part.
0.5: The plan assumes control over independent owners, or the acceptance test cannot run with one participant missing.
0: No workable cross-owner plan, or one that depends on authority nobody has.
BOUND · 1 point1: States relevant operating assumptions, scope and an observable trigger to reconsider the claim.
0.75: A relevant boundary and trigger, but one is imprecise—for example, a trigger that cannot be observed as written.
0.5: The boundary is generic (“if problems occur”) or does not match the claim and the plan.
0: No boundary, or a boundary that contradicts the case facts.
ACT + EVIDENCE · 1 point1: Gives a feasible bounded action, named responsibility and inspectable evidence, while distinguishing completed from proposed verification.
0.75: A feasible action and useful evidence, but one element is missing—for example, the responsible role, or whether the evidence is completed or proposed.
0.5: The action is vague or not feasible in the case, or the evidence cannot be inspected by someone else.
0: No action, or evidence that is invented or reported as completed when it is only proposed.
REFIT · 1 point1: Explains how the new evidence affects the initial claim and justifies RETAIN, REVISE or REPLACE with a coherent final record.
0.75: The effect of the new evidence and the RETAIN / REVISE / REPLACE choice are justified, but the final record leaves one inconsistency with the new boundary.
0.5: A choice is stated but not justified from the new evidence, or the final record repeats Part A without addressing the change.
0: No REFIT, or a response that ignores or contradicts the new evidence.

Different practice example · weak: “Every API passes, so the shared service is ready.”

Stronger: For a different city dashboard, acceptance includes timestamps, feed loss and independent version changes. The coordinator records what each owner agreed to and what remains useful without one feed.

2 · PART A · 20 MINUTES

Assessed scenario · fictional teaching case

A fictional university plans a shared incident dashboard using campus security, transport and facilities systems. Each system remains operational on its own and has a different owner and release schedule. Security supplies incident identifiers; transport supplies vehicle locations with timestamps; facilities supplies building-access status. The owners agree to a limited daytime pilot, but no agreement defines a common freshness limit or how to display an unavailable feed. Local API tests passed. No cross-system failure rehearsal has been performed.

Teaching simulation. Use only the supplied facts. You may calculate from them. Do not invent additional test results, thresholds or completed audits. If you propose extra evidence or a new threshold, label it proposed.

Keep the complete written response to about 180–260 focused words; use compact tables or notation. Include the source application and transfer answer in that budget. No external experiment is required. State facts, assumptions and proposed evidence separately. Quote or cite the assigned source precisely; explain its role. Report actual study time at the end.

A1 · FIT · 1 POINT

Classify the SoS and identify managerial and operational independence. Name one shared service that creates value for the pilot.

A2 · INTEGRATE · 1 POINT

Propose an interface/governance record for two constituents and a staged acceptance test across them. Include ownership, version/freshness assumptions and useful behavior when one feed is absent.

A3 · BOUND · 1 POINT

Separate what the pilot owner can control from what requires agreement. State a trigger for suspending or narrowing the shared claim.

A4 · ACT + EVIDENCE · 1 POINT

Recommend pilot, restricted pilot or hold. Name the responsible coordination role, participating owners and evidence needed for the next stage.

Name the relevant test/log/audit, the conditions it must cover, who checks it, and what evidence is still missing. Do not describe requested evidence as if it already exists.
