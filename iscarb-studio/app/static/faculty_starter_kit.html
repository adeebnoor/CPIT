This starter kit is the operational contract for one faculty member preparing one complete 90-minute university lecture. It is designed to preserve the technical source while raising reasoning, evidence, accountability and readiness.

1. The non-negotiable contract

90 minutesOne complete live lecture.
20 unitsExactly twenty teaching units.
Full P1 coverageNo major primary topic is silently deferred.
One decision threadThe opening problem returns throughout the lecture.
Source rule: technical claims in the weekly-source channel must be supported by the supplied lecture bundle. ISCARB pedagogy and contextual enrichment are separate channels and must not be disguised as source content.

2. The four teaching phases

IFHAM · Understand

Frame the professional responsibility, map the domain, expose the five CLOs and six H-Stack capabilities, then force prediction before explanation.

MARIS · Practice

Build mechanism understanding, compare alternatives, quantify trade-offs, test claims, surface uncertainty and end with a senior-design challenge.

ATQAN · Master

Apply the weekly idea in a Saudi-aligned context, connect accountability and practitioner wellbeing, examine trends, and enforce critical AI literacy.

MAYYIZ · Discern

Launch the portfolio task, mutate constraints, demand proof of capability, score with a capability rubric, and close with a bounded assurance case.

4. Evidence before badges

ISCARB Verified

A verified release requires source fidelity, engineering rigor, cumulative fidelity, readiness evidence and provenance separation. BLOCKED means “review required,” not “failure of the whole lecture.”

Readiness discipline

Map only outcomes that the lecture and student evidence fully support. A shared keyword is not attainment. The current public implementation carries the ETEC Information Technology 2025 readiness pack; additional disciplines require validated standards packs.

5. Faculty pilot

01Choose one existing lecture
02Compile and inspect the preview
03Teach it once
04Record what helped / hindered
05Repeat only if it earned reuse

Recommended evaluation questions: preparation time, fidelity to the original lecture, student engagement, quality of student reasoning, usefulness of the evidence/rubric, and whether the faculty member voluntarily chooses to reuse the workflow.

6. Master prompt for a single 90-minute lecture

You are an expert in curriculum engineering and instructional design working under ISCARB (Intelligent Systems for Culturally Aligned Readiness Blueprints). TASK Transform the supplied PRIMARY weekly lecture into exactly ONE complete 90-minute lecture consisting of exactly 20 Units. Cover every major technical topic family in the primary lecture. Supporting sources may clarify, verify, contextualize, or deepen P1; they may not silently replace P1 or expand mandatory scope. SOURCE FIDELITY Separate all output into three provenance channels: 1) WEEKLY-SOURCE TECHNICAL CONTENT — only technical claims supported by the supplied lecture bundle. 2) ISCARB PEDAGOGY / DECISION WORK — CLOs, H-Stack, prediction, Socratic challenges, evidence policy, rubric, assurance structure. 3) CONTEXTUAL ENRICHMENT — Saudi context, contemporary trends, practitioner wellbeing, AI literacy, readiness alignment, or external examples not present in P1. Label these clearly and do not attribute them to P1. PEDAGOGICAL CONTRACT - Start with one ill-structured engineering crisis specific to this lecture. - Maintain one engineering decision thread through Unit 20. - Teach from first principles: mechanism, assumptions, failure modes, constraints. - Any design choice must expose a hard trade-off rather than present one “best practice.” - Prediction must occur before explanatory content where required. - All major P1 technical topic families must first be taught by Unit 15. - Do not introduce a new major technical solution in Units 16–20. - Use bounded assurance language; never claim zero risk, guaranteed security, or absolute proof. 20 UNITS 01 Title + professional/ethical responsibility + crisis 02 Domain Spine 03 Exactly five measurable CLOs 04 Six H-Stack capabilities: analytical thinking, engineering judgment, evidence-based thinking, socio-technical thinking, risk-aware design, ethical responsibility 05 Predict → Constraint → Derive → Name 06 First-principles mechanism 07 Architecture / implementation 08 Alternatives + quantified trade-off 09 Measurement + falsification 10 Known / Unknown / Decision-sensitive unknown / What we monitor 11 Saudi-aligned context 12 Accountability annotations 13 Trend 14 Practitioner wellbeing 15 Critical AI literacy: AI MAY ASSIST / AI MUST NOT BE TRUSTED AUTONOMOUSLY + audit workflow 16 Portfolio task launch 17 Constraint mutation 18 Evidence policy: Claim / Evidence / Warrant / Counter-evidence / Residual uncertainty 19 Four-level capability rubric covering technical correctness, first principles, trade-offs, falsification, constraint adaptation, readiness/professional accountability 20 Bounded assurance case ending in APPROVE / CONDITIONALLY APPROVE / REDESIGN / REJECT READINESS Use only the validated readiness standard supplied by the system. Map an SLO/KLO only when every required component is taught and evidenced. Partial overlap is not attainment. OUTPUT QUALITY Presenter content should be concise and visual; detailed explanations belong in the detailed deck and instructor guide. Prefer diagrams for structure, matrices for decisions, process flows for mechanisms, and minimal text for principles.
