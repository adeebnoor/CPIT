STUDENT GUIDE

One familiar rhythm.
A new engineering challenge.

Each chapter follows the same path: learn the mechanism in class, review the named source, then apply it to a new case in the assignment.

Use the lectures

  1. Start with the chapter map. Slide 2 connects the five concepts around one question. Slide 3 introduces the fictional case that the whole chapter keeps returning to.
  2. Read the takeaway and the points. Each concept has a diagram or original source figure, a one-line takeaway and short points under WHAT IT MEANS.
  3. Open Full explanation for depth. It explains the concept from the textbook and links to the original slides.
  4. Try before you reveal. Answer the slide question yourself, then open Model answer. At the three stations, think, discuss with a partner, then update your decision card.

What is assessed

Assessment scope: the five chapter objectives, the classroom concepts and the named required source review. Toolkit activities are optional unless your instructor assigns them. Any extra required topic will be announced in Blackboard before it is assessed.

  1. Required source review. In each chapter, open Study & tools → Required source review. It names two source selections, usually 8–12 slides, and links to each original slide.
  2. Five-objective practice. Answer the five practice questions before the assignment and the next class. They are written for this course, are not graded and are not exam items; review any you miss.
  3. Assessed assignment. Apply one assigned source concept to the assignment artifact and answer the short transfer question. Both count within the existing rubric points.
  4. Explain it in class. Your instructor may ask you to explain a changed example. Practice answers are public, so they are not a secure test.

How the assignment works

  1. Enter through the course page. Open the assignment from the learning path and use the same Student ID and browser each time. Drafts stay on your device; this page does not send them to the instructor.
  2. Part A: your first judgment. Write FIT, the chapter artifact, BOUND, ACT and EVIDENCE, then commit. Committing freezes Part A; it is not submission.
  3. Read STRESS. Assignments 1–2: after you commit, the new evidence opens automatically. From Assignment 3: save the committed Part A record, upload it in Blackboard, open the new-evidence item Blackboard then releases, and paste it into the assignment page. Part A stays saved and read-only; continue with REFIT below. If you were waiting for a code, save or download a backup and refresh the same assignment using the same browser and Student ID.
  4. Part B: REFIT. Decide whether the new evidence leaves your claim intact, under pressure or crossed, then justify RETAIN, REVISE or REPLACE. A well-justified RETAIN earns the same credit as a change.
  5. Declare, export, submit. Declare any AI assistance, export the PDF, check it and upload it to Blackboard. Exporting here is not submission.

Each rubric row explains what 1, 0.75, 0.5 and 0 mean for that criterion. Assignment 1 is worth 4 points; Assignments 2–9 are worth 5 points each.

Assignments 3–9, step by step

From Assignment 3 the method stays the same, with three additions: you build and test a small piece of Python in every assignment, the new evidence arrives through Blackboard, and you may be asked to explain your work in two minutes. Plan about 80–90 minutes in total.

  1. Prepare (about 15 minutes). Attend or open the lecture, read the two named source selections, and answer the five-objective practice. The assignment checks that you cite a slide from the assigned reading.
  2. Part A, your first judgment (about 20 minutes, 120–180 words; code is not counted). Write FIT (the problem that governs the decision), the chapter artifact (for example TRACE, TEST or RECOVER), BOUND (the observable condition where your claim stops holding), and ACT + EVIDENCE (what you would do, who owns it, and what someone else could check). Use only the supplied facts, and label anything you propose as proposed.
  3. Build it in Python (every assignment from 3 to 9; about 30 minutes). Write your build plan first. The page gives starter code: complete it (a fault tree in Assignment 3, an access rule in 4, a reconciliation in 5, an evidence-only fit check in 6, an adapter in 7, an idempotent service in 8, an honest display in 9) and write at least three tests of your own, including one case that must be refused or must fail safely. From Assignment 4 your tests are tested too: the course runs them against known buggy versions, and each bug must make one of your tests fail. Read the short primary source named on the page first (about 15 minutes) and use it in your plan. Select Run my tests and the course checks: Python runs in your browser, nothing is installed or uploaded, and the first run downloads Python once (about 10 MB). Keep a failing check if you cannot fix it, and explain it. Changing the code clears the record, so run again before you commit. Your written Part A must use what the build showed.
  4. Different formats. Assignment 6 gives you a colleague’s recommendation to critique, and Assignment 9 another team’s design to review. Answer the same five criteria, and say what you would sign instead.
  5. Commit Part A. Check every field, then commit. Part A becomes read-only. Committing is not submission.
  6. Upload Part A to Blackboard. Select Print / save Part A as PDF on the assignment page, then upload that file to Assignment N · Part A (committed record) in Blackboard.
  7. Get the new evidence. Blackboard then shows Assignment N · New evidence. Copy all of its text, paste it into the assignment page and select Check and continue. If the page says the text does not match, copy the whole item again. If you continue anyway, your record is marked unverified.
  8. Part B, REFIT (about 10 minutes). Say whether your original boundary is intact, under pressure or crossed. Choose RETAIN, REVISE or REPLACE, justify it from the new evidence, and write the final decision record. A well-justified RETAIN earns the same credit as a change.
  9. Declare AI use and submit. Say which tools you used, for what, and what you checked yourself, or write “No AI used.” Export the final PDF, check it, and submit it in Blackboard before the deadline.
  10. Ownership check. Your instructor may ask a few students, chosen at random, to explain their Part A and REFIT in about two minutes. Knowing your own reasoning is all the preparation it needs.

Common mistakes

  • Citing a slide that is not in the assigned reading.
  • Presenting a proposed test as if it had been run.
  • A boundary such as “if problems occur” instead of something that can be observed.
  • Pasting only part of the Blackboard evidence.
  • Changing your code after running it; the page asks you to run it again before you can commit.
  • Tests that only check the easy case. At least one of yours must try something that has to be refused or has to fail safely.

Use the same browser and Student ID throughout; drafts stay on your device. Download a backup before leaving a shared computer.

Workload, feedback and saved work

Plan about 15–25 minutes for the reading and practice questions, and 35–55 minutes in total including the assignment for Assignments 1 and 2. From Assignment 3, plan about 80–90 minutes, including about 30 minutes for the Python build that runs in your browser. Report your actual time. Aim for 120–180 focused words; your code, tests and build record replace paragraphs.

After feedback, use the feedback and revision fields, correct or defend your final record, and export again if your instructor asks. Your first export is kept for comparison.

Lecture cards and assignment drafts are stored only in this browser, separated by chapter and Student ID. Download a backup before you use a shared computer, and do not clear site data before exporting. Started an earlier edition? Use the previous-edition link inside the assignment to finish or export it with the same ID and browser.

Offline: download the chapter ZIP from the learning path, extract all of it, then open START-HERE.html. Keep its folders together.
