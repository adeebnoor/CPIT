PRACTICE CASE · NOT ASSESSED

Fictional campus Wi-Fi assistant

A fictional campus operations team uses an AI assistant to recommend RESET, ESCALATE or WAIT during Wi-Fi incidents. Two dashboards are available, but both use the same model, prompt and retrieval source. Operators can ignore the recommendation. The team has tested ordinary daytime incidents but has not tested an AI outage or contradictory recommendations during peak registration.

Your practice decision: should the assistant control the action, remain advisory-only, or be disabled during the peak window?

What a weak answer looks like

“Use the AI because it is fast, but keep a backup and test it more.”

This is too generic. It does not name the shared AI dependency, the boundary, inspectable evidence, or who owns the decision.

What a strong Part A looks like

FIT + AI mechanism

The two dashboards do not provide AI diversity because both depend on the same model, prompt and retrieval source. A shared AI error can therefore affect both views. Answer availability is not enough to establish dependable decision support.

BOUND + evidence

Keep the claim limited to model version practice-v3, ordinary daytime incidents, and mandatory operator confirmation. Peak registration, model changes, retrieval changes, contradictory outputs or AI unavailability reopen the decision. Required evidence includes versioned replay tests and an AI-unavailable drill.

ACT + responsibility

Use the assistant as ADVISORY-ONLY. The duty engineer owns the action; the service owner reviews any wider authority. No recommendation should directly execute a reset until independent checks are tested under peak-like conditions.

Evidence honesty

The existing daytime test is observed. The proposed peak-load, contradictory-output and AI-unavailable tests are proposed, not passed evidence. Record the tested model version, conditions, result and reviewer.

COMMIT

Why Part A becomes read-only

Commit preserves your first engineering judgment before new evidence appears. It is not Blackboard submission. After Commit, do not rewrite Part A to make yourself look correct; use REFIT to show how your reasoning changes.

STRESS · PRACTICE

New evidence appears

During a rehearsal, two operators receive different recommendations for the same incident after the retrieval index is refreshed. The dashboards are both online, but the AI advice is inconsistent.

REFIT example

Boundary: CROSSED. The original advisory-only decision assumed operator confirmation but did not yet have evidence about contradictory outputs after retrieval changes. Keep direct control disabled, suspend use during the peak window until the retrieval version is pinned and regression-tested, and require one authoritative non-AI operating rule for the immediate action. The service owner must review the regression evidence before restoring advisory use.

WHAT YOU SUBMIT

One JSON file in Blackboard

  1. Finish all required fields, STRESS → REFIT, AI-use declaration and human review.
  2. When the page says the record is complete, click Download Blackboard JSON.
  3. Upload the .json file to the matching Blackboard assignment.
  4. Open Blackboard and verify the submission receipt/timestamp.

Do not submit screenshots or an AI chat transcript. PDF and Markdown exports are optional personal copies unless the instructor explicitly asks for them.

AFTER BLACKBOARD

What happens to your submission

  1. The JSON is checked for the correct assignment schema, Student ID, Commit record and human-review attestation.
  2. Your reasoning is scored against the exact rubric shown on the assignment page.
  3. Fluent wording, length or generic “AI ethics” language does not earn points by itself.
  4. Contradictory, unclear, invented-evidence or low-confidence cases are sent to the instructor for human review.
  5. Blackboard remains the official grade and feedback record.

Research basis

Before you submit: 30-second check

  • Did I answer the AI-containing system case, not write generic AI advice?
  • Did I separate observed/derived evidence from proposed/unknown evidence?
  • Did I name the operating boundary and what would reopen the decision?
  • Did I preserve Part A before STRESS and explain my REFIT?
  • Did I download the real Blackboard JSON and verify the Blackboard receipt?
