What good FBR reasoning looks like

This neutral example teaches the reasoning pattern without revealing the answer to Chapter 10 or 11.

Scenario: A campus access-control service loses its central network connection. Local controllers can continue using a short-lived credential cache, but revocation data will become stale if the outage continues.
FITUse availability with controlled graceful degradation: keep low-risk access operating because complete shutdown would create a larger operational disruption.
BOUNDThis remains fit only while the credential cache is less than 15 minutes old, local controllers remain healthy, and no revocation warning is active. Reopen the decision if any of those conditions fail.
STRESSThe network outage reaches 40 minutes and the revocation feed is now stale. The original applicability boundary has been crossed.
REFITREVISE. Keep cached entry only for low-risk zones, require supervised manual verification for sensitive zones, log every override, and return to normal policy only after revocation checks are restored.
Pattern to notice: FIT names the knowledge that is appropriate now; BOUND states an observable condition that can invalidate it; STRESS changes the situation; REFIT explicitly decides whether to retain, revise, or replace the original action.

Evaluation rubric · 4 points

1FIT
1BOUND
1ACT + EVIDENCE
1REFIT

The score rewards the quality of professional judgment, not length, confidence, polished English, or simply filling every box.

Criterion1 point · defensiblePartial / weak evidence
FITSelects the relevant mechanism or property and explains why it fits this specific professional use now.Names a concept but gives a generic definition, weak context link, or no real selection rationale.
BOUNDStates an observable applicability boundary and a clear trigger for reopening the decision.Uses vague language such as “if things change” without a measurable or inspectable condition.
ACT + EVIDENCEProduces a concrete professional action/artifact and names evidence that would make it defensible to another professional.Gives an intention without an actionable artifact, owner, measurement, or inspectable evidence.
REFITExplains whether STRESS crossed the stated boundary, then rationally retains, revises, or replaces the original action.Changes the answer without referring to the boundary, or keeps the answer despite material contradictory evidence.

AI / LLM policy

AI may assist your work; it may not replace your professional judgment.
  • Permitted uses include language editing, clarification, and brainstorming.
  • You remain fully responsible for every submitted FIT, BOUND, ACT, EVIDENCE, and REFIT decision.
  • You must be able to explain and defend every sentence you submit, even if AI helped you produce or edit it.
  • In the LMS submission comment, include one line exactly in this form: AI use: None / Language-editing / Brainstorming / Other: ____.
  • Do not submit an LLM answer that you cannot independently justify. A polished answer that you cannot defend does not demonstrate ownership of the judgment.

Oral ownership verification

  • Students may be selected randomly for a 60–120 second micro-viva in the following class.
  • You may be asked why you selected a particular FIT, where your BOUND came from, what evidence would change your decision, or how you would respond to a new mutation.
  • Short in-class no-device FBR micro-cases may also be used periodically to confirm independent performance.
  • If you cannot explain or defend a material part of your own submission, the submission is marked verification incomplete until a short reassessment is completed. This is not an automatic accusation of AI misuse; it is a check that you own the judgment you submitted.

Submission demonstrates the judgment. Viva verifies ownership of the judgment.
