ILLUSTRATIVE WORKED EXAMPLE · NOT AN ANSWER KEY

What complete FBR reasoning looks like

This neutral example teaches the full pattern without revealing the answer to any assessed chapter.

Scenario: A campus access-control service loses its central network connection. Assumption: the approved operating policy permits cached credentials for no more than 15 minutes while local controllers remain healthy and no revocation alert is active.
FITUse controlled graceful degradation to preserve low-risk access temporarily rather than forcing a total shutdown.
BOUNDThis remains fit only while the approved 15-minute cache window is not exceeded, controllers remain healthy, and no revocation alert appears. Reopen the decision if any condition fails.
ACTIssue a temporary degraded-mode instruction: low-risk doors may use cached credentials; sensitive zones require manual verification.
EVIDENCERecord controller health, cache age, revocation-feed status, the duty manager approving degraded mode, and an audit log of every manual override.
STRESSThe outage reaches 40 minutes and revocation data is stale. The stated boundary is crossed.
REFITREVISE. Stop cached access except for explicitly approved low-risk zones, require supervised verification elsewhere, preserve override logs, and restore normal policy only after revocation checks recover.
Pattern: FIT selects what is appropriate now; BOUND states when that choice stops being fit; ACT creates a professional artifact; EVIDENCE makes it defensible; STRESS changes the situation; REFIT explicitly retains, revises, or replaces the action.

Evaluation rubric · 4 points

1FIT
1BOUND
1ACT + EVIDENCE
1REFIT

The score rewards defensible professional judgment, not length, confidence, polished English, or simply filling every box.

Criterion1 point · defensiblePartial / weak evidence
FITSelects the relevant mechanism or property and explains why it fits this specific professional use now.Names a concept but gives a generic definition, weak context link, or no selection rationale.
BOUNDStates an observable applicability boundary and a clear trigger for reopening the decision.Uses vague language such as “if things change” without an inspectable condition.
ACT + EVIDENCEProduces a concrete professional action/artifact and names evidence that would make it defensible to another professional.Gives an intention without an actionable artifact, owner, measurement, or inspectable evidence.
REFITExplains whether STRESS crossed the stated boundary, then rationally retains, revises, or replaces the original action.Changes the answer without referring to the boundary, or retains it despite material contradictory evidence.

AI / LLM policy

AI may assist your work; it may not replace your professional judgment.
  • Permitted uses include language editing, clarification, and brainstorming.
  • You remain responsible for every submitted FIT, BOUND, ACT, EVIDENCE, and REFIT decision.
  • You must be able to explain and defend every sentence you submit.
  • In the LMS submission comment, include: AI use: None / Language-editing / Brainstorming / Other: ____.

Oral ownership verification

  • Students may be selected randomly for a 60–120 second micro-viva in the following class.
  • You may be asked why you selected a FIT, where your BOUND came from, what evidence would change your decision, or how you would respond to a mutation.
  • If you cannot explain or defend a material part of your submission, it is marked verification incomplete until a short reassessment is completed.

Submission demonstrates the judgment. Viva verifies ownership of the judgment.
