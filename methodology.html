A FACULTY-ADOPTABLE PEDAGOGICAL FRAMEWORK

Teach knowledge. Transfer judgment.

ISCARB is a split-delivery framework for university teaching in the GenAI era. It separates disciplinary instruction from bounded decision practice, requires inspectable evidence, and keeps final professional responsibility with the human learner.

PEDAGOGICAL MANIFESTO

Why ISCARB now?

Generative AI can produce plausible explanations, code and polished prose. The educational problem therefore shifts from “Can a student produce an answer?” to “Can a student own, bound and defend a consequential decision?”

ISCARB treats professional judgment as an observable learning outcome. A student may use tools to prepare evidence, but the student must still state what fits, where it stops fitting, what action follows, what evidence supports that action, and what new condition would force a change. The final sign-off remains human.
01

Split delivery

Use class time to teach the disciplinary mechanism. Move the consequential decision into a separate bounded task rather than mixing explanation and assessment into one activity.

02

Evidence before confidence

Students do not earn credit merely for sounding certain. They expose assumptions, measurable boundaries, counter-evidence and inspectable artifacts.

03

Human sign-off

AI may draft, test, summarize or probe. It does not own certification, approval, risk acceptance, assurance or the final professional verdict.

THE REASONING GRAMMAR

FBR + STRESS

FITSelect the mechanism or option that is justified now.
BOUNDName the observable condition beyond which that choice stops being fit.
ACTTurn the judgment into a concrete professional action or artifact.
EVIDENCEProvide evidence another professional can independently inspect.
REFITAfter a changed condition, retain, revise or replace the decision.
STRESS is sequential by design. The learner commits the initial reasoning before seeing the changed condition. This makes assumptions and boundaries visible before hindsight can rewrite them.
INSTRUCTOR IMPLEMENTATION GUIDE

Apply ISCARB in any technical course

The framework is intentionally discipline-agnostic. The course supplies its own subject matter; ISCARB supplies the decision architecture.

1

Teach the mechanism In-Class

Identify what students must understand before judgment is possible: model, process, architecture, metric, hazard, algorithm, policy or design principle. Build the lecture around source figures, worked examples and explicit trade-offs.

2

Convert it into an FBR decision

Write a realistic case with incomplete but sufficient information. Require FIT, BOUND, ACT and EVIDENCE. Avoid questions that can be completed by paraphrasing the lecture or prompting an AI for a generic answer.

3

Verify ownership

Introduce STRESS, then use a short rubric and selective oral verification. Ask the learner to explain what changed, whether the boundary was crossed, and why the final verdict remains defensible.

LARGE COHORTS

Viva without exhausting the instructor

Rotating spot-checkOrally verify a changing sample of students each week rather than interviewing the whole cohort every time.
Risk-triggered vivaPrioritize unusual, internally inconsistent, highly polished or evidence-poor submissions for human verification.
2–3 minute micro-vivaUse the same three prompts: claim, boundary, evidence. Short fixed prompts improve consistency and throughput.
Calibrated panelsFor large classes, trained TAs or faculty panels can use the same rubric with periodic moderation and audit samples.

These are scalable implementation patterns, not claims that one sampling rate fits every course. Each instructor should set sampling intensity according to class size, assessment stakes and institutional policy.

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE & LEARNING ANALYTICS

Evidence before institutional claims

A framework intended for broader adoption must show more than an attractive implementation. It needs reproducible learning evidence.

Evidence program

  • Learning-outcome performance before/after or with appropriate comparison designs.
  • Quality of boundary-setting and evidence use across repeated FBR tasks.
  • Student confidence in independent engineering judgment.
  • Micro-viva agreement between written work and oral ownership.
  • Instructor workload and scalability in large cohorts.

Current evidence status

ISCARB is implemented as a live student-facing course system in CPIT-455. This page does not claim cross-institution causal effectiveness or universal superiority. Such claims should follow a defined evaluation protocol, transparent measures and replicable data.

Adoption principle: publish the framework early enough to be tested, but reserve strong effectiveness claims for evidence that can survive independent scrutiny.
INSTITUTIONAL ADOPTION LADDER

From one course to a university model

Course pilot

One instructor, one course, explicit CLO mapping, a small number of FBR tasks, and documented workload.

Department pilot

Multiple instructors across different technical subjects use a shared rubric and common implementation log.

Multi-institution replication

Independent teams implement the same reasoning grammar, report deviations, and compare learning and workload evidence.
